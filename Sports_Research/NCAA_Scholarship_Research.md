## 1. NCAA Scholarship Structure — Key Concepts

### Headcount vs. Equivalency (Critical Distinction)

**Headcount sports** (football, basketball, women's volleyball at D1): Each scholarship must be a **full grant-in-aid** — cannot be split. Athlete gets everything or nothing.

**Equivalency sports** (most other sports): Total scholarship budget can be divided any way among any number of athletes. Coach can offer 25–50% scholarships to promising athletes. **Far more accessible for international athletes.**

All D2 sports and most NAIA sports are **equivalency** regardless of D1 headcount status.

## 3. Deep Dive — Top 4 Opportunities

### #1 — Men's Wrestling (D2 / NAIA)

**Why it's #1:**
- Weight classes 125 lb (56.7 kg) and 133 lb (60.3 kg) are **chronically undersupplied** domestically — these exactly match 58–64 kg Nahua male physiques
- Many D2 and NAIA programs carry **open scholarships** in these weight classes every year
- Equivalency sport: coach can offer 25–50% to try an athlete out, increase as they prove themselves
- Freestyle/Greco-Roman background transfers well to NCAA folkstyle (one season adaptation)
- Altitude-trained aerobic capacity = major advantage in a demanding 7-minute match
- ~78 D1 programs + ~90 D2 + ~55 NAIA = large coaching pool actively recruiting

**How to get discovered:**
- UWW-sanctioned competition results (even regional) create a searchable international profile
- 3–5 minute YouTube highlight reel + direct coach email = most effective D2/NAIA tool
- Beat the Streets (US wrestling NGO) has international connections
- Wrestling coaches are uniquely receptive to cold-contact outreach vs. higher-profile sports

**Performance standards:**
| Level | Standard |
|-------|----------|
| D1 Mid-Major | Pan-American Games qualifier or Mexican national team |
| D2 Scholarship | U20 state/regional champion in Mexico |
| NAIA Scholarship | Consistent regional competitor |

---

### #2/#3 — Distance Running (D2 / NAIA)

**Why altitude is a known, recruitable asset:**
- Communities at 1,200–2,700 m (Zongolica VZ, Sierra Alta HGO) produce measurably higher hemoglobin and VO2 max
- US coaches **know this** — they actively seek altitude-adapted athletes
- A sub-15:00 5K from a 2,000 m community is worth more to a coach than sub-14:45 from sea level

**Performance standards:**
| Level | Men's 5K | Men's 8K XC | Women's 5K | Women's 6K XC |
|-------|----------|-------------|------------|---------------|
| D1 Mid-Major | Sub-14:00 | Sub-24:30 | Sub-16:00 | Sub-21:00 |
| D2 Scholarship | Sub-14:30 | Sub-25:30 | Sub-17:00 | Sub-22:00 |
| NAIA Scholarship | Sub-15:00 | Sub-26:30 | Sub-17:30 | Sub-23:00 |

**How to get discovered:**
- Register all athletes on World Athletics (worldathletics.org) — any FMAA-sanctioned race creates a permanent searchable record at zero cost
- GPS-tracked training log as supplementary evidence to coaches
- Direct coach email with documented times and altitude of training location

---

### #4 — Cross Country

- D2/NAIA programs consistently **cannot fill full rosters** with scholarship-level athletes
- Highland terrain training (Sierra de Zongolica, Sierra Alta Hidalgo) is directly relevant to US XC courses
- A sub-26:00 8K male from a rural highland community is recruitble at D2 without question
- Funded from the same T&F scholarship pool — coaches value dedicated XC athletes

---

### #6 — Women's Rowing Coxswain (Unusual but Sound)

**The counterintuitive opportunity:**
- Women's rowing was expanded by Title IX — 90+ D1 programs have 20.0 equivalencies each
- Chronic shortage of athletes; most programs recruit complete novices and teach rowing in college
- **Coxswain** (the one who steers and calls cadence — does NOT row) is specifically recruited to be **small and light**: under 50 kg, 155 cm or shorter
- A Nahua woman with leadership presence and the right physique (very common) is genuinely competitive
- **No prior rowing experience required or expected**
- Requires: English development, introductory rowing concepts (video study), contact with D2 rowing coaches

## 4. Academic Eligibility — Critical Barriers and Workarounds

### NCAA D1 — Most Difficult
- 16 core courses required
- SAT/ACT required (sliding scale with GPA)
- Mexican preparatoria transcripts accepted after NCAA Eligibility Center evaluation
- Test centers: Pachuca (HGO), Xalapa (VZ); cost ~$55–100 USD per attempt
- Telebachillerato transcripts generally accepted but need to map to core course requirements

### NCAA D2 — More Accessible
- 14 core courses required
- **SAT/ACT NOT required** (removed in 2016)
- 2.2 GPA minimum in core courses (~7.5/10 on Mexican scale)
- Significantly more realistic for rural community graduates

### NAIA — Most Accessible ⭐ (Primary Target)
- No SAT/ACT requirement
- ~2.0 GPA minimum (~6.5–7.0/10 Mexican scale)
- Individualized review process; coaches help athletes navigate documentation
- Many NAIA schools (often religiously affiliated: Concordia, Doane, MidAmerica Nazarene) have established Latin American relationships
- **NAIA should be the primary target; D2 the aspirational step-up**

### JUCO Bridge (Best Workaround)
1. Enroll in US 2-year community college (no SAT/ACT, NJCAA rules)
2. Compete 1–2 years, establish US academic record
3. Transfer to NCAA D1/D2 with eligibility clock intact
**This is the most common pathway for Mexican athletes without standardized test scores.**

### NCAA Eligibility Center — Start Early
- Athletes can register at age **14–15** (early registration accepted)
- Registration fee: $100 USD (fee waivers available for financial need documentation)
- Start early to identify and fix academic gaps before graduation

## 5. Sport-Program Design Implications

### What Changes Based on This Research

| Previous assumption | Corrected finding |
|--------------------|------------------|
| Judo = good college pathway | Judo has NO NCAA scholarships — club sport only |
| Boxing = good college pathway | Boxing has NO NCAA scholarships — club sport only |
| Weightlifting = college pathway | Olympic weightlifting has NO NCAA scholarships |
| Taekwondo = college pathway | Taekwondo has NO NCAA scholarships |
| All good Olympic sports = college scholarships | Only wrestling (combat) and track/field (running) have NCAA scholarships |

### Revised Sport Priority (for US College Pathway)

**For college scholarship pipeline specifically:**
1. **Wrestling** — only combat sport with NCAA scholarships; best physical fit; highest domestic gap
2. **Track & Field / Cross Country (distance)** — altitude advantage is recruitable; large scholarship pool
3. **Soccer** — active Latin American pipeline; NAIA especially accessible

**For overall program development (Olympic + college):**
- Wrestling, boxing, judo remain for Olympic development
- Only wrestling has a direct college scholarship pathway
- Athletes in boxing/judo could be offered academic scholarships as non-athletes, or convert to wrestling

## 6. Recommended Program Actions

### Year 1–2
1. Establish UWW-affiliated wrestling club (prerequisite for NCAA-recruitble results)
2. Register all runners on World Athletics — free, permanent, searchable
3. Open NCAA Eligibility Center accounts for athletes at age 14–15
4. Map telebachillerato course selection to NCAA core course requirements
5. Build D2/NAIA coach contact database (30–50 wrestling + 20–30 XC programs); direct email outreach costs nothing

### Year 3–5
1. Target PANAM Junior Wrestling Championships — clearest signal to US coaches
2. Establish formal road race calendar in FMAA-sanctioned 5K/10K events (twice yearly minimum)
3. Develop bilingual (English/Spanish) athlete profile template — photo, results, academic status, contact
4. First NAIA scholarship offers expected for top performers at this stage

### Academic Investment (Highest Leverage)
- The bottleneck is **not athletic performance** — it is **academic documentation**
- Ensure transcripts are formatted for recognition: school stamp, official grades, teacher signatures
- Budget $100/athlete for NCAA EC registration (or obtain fee waivers)
- For D1/D2 targets: SAT prep investment (~$300–500 for prep course)
