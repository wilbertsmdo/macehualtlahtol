## Overview

Research project to identify the best Olympic sports for a youth development program targeting Nahua/Macehuali communities in Veracruz and Hidalgo, Mexico. Two research axes: (1) lowest-cost Olympic sports accessible with limited municipal budgets, and (2) physical/physiological profile of Macehuali youth and their natural sports suitability.

- **Iron deficiency deep-dive** — clarified that causes are multi-factorial: dietary erosion (primary), absorption inhibitors (phytates in maize, tannins), parasitic load (hookworm — significant in lowland VZ), and increased demand in adolescent girls/women; practical protocol: deworm first, then supplement, pair iron foods with Vitamin C

### Current state

- `Sports_Research_Report.md` — complete foundational research document covering all requested topics
- Key findings documented:
  - **Top sports for Macehuali youth profile:** Wrestling, Boxing (48–60 kg), Judo, Weightlifting (Tier 1); Middle-distance running + road cycling for highland communities (Tier 2)
  - **Cheapest sports overall:** Distance running ($200–$400/3yr), Judo ($350–$700), Wrestling ($400–$850)
  - **Full program for 60 athletes across 3 sports:** $15,000–$25,000 USD startup
  - **Critical warning:** 34–44% stunting rate + 20–35% iron deficiency anemia — screening protocol required before any fitness testing
  - **Altitude asset:** Highland communities (Zongolica VZ, Sierra Alta HGO) have natural hematological adaptations equivalent to elite altitude training camps

## Checkpoint — 2026-09-25 (Session 2)

### Work completed this session

- **NCAA Scholarship Research** (`NCAA_Scholarship_Research.md`) — identified top scholarship opportunities for Nahua athletes in US colleges; critical finding: judo, boxing, weightlifting, and taekwondo have ZERO NCAA scholarships (club sports only); only wrestling among combat sports has NCAA scholarships; ranked top opportunities: wrestling D2/NAIA (#1), distance running D2/NAIA (#2/#3), cross country (#4), women's rowing coxswain (#6 — no prior experience required)

- **NCAA Understaffed Programs** (`NCAA_Understaffed_Programs.md`) — ranked top 15 chronically undersupplied programs regardless of Nahua relevance; #1 women's rowing (Title IX expansion, novice model, ~40–50% fill rate with experienced rowers); #2 men's gymnastics (collapsed from ~80 to 16 D1 programs due to supply failure); documented structural reasons for each gap (thin pipeline, geographic concentration, Title IX without pipeline development, pro pathway diversion)

- **Other Opportunities** (`Other_Opportunities.md`) — full priority matrix of non-NCAA pathways; key finding: SEDENA military athlete program (MXN $9,500–$12,000/month + full medical) is the most underused pathway for 18–22 year olds without international results yet; documented stacked scholarship model: Beca Bienestar + UV/UAEH athletic + IDERV/CODEH = MXN $5,400–$9,400/month; IOC Olympic Solidarity LA 2028 cycle now active

- **Sport-for-Development Evidence Base** (`Sport_as_Development_Research.md`) — comprehensive review of academic literature; key findings: (1) "sport-plus" model (explicit life skills instruction) works; "plus-sport" (assumed emergence) does not; (2) Clontarf Foundation (Australia) is the direct design reference — "no school → no training" inversion; (3) cultural identity strength is the single strongest protective factor for indigenous youth; (4) ejido/cargo system maps directly to Bolivian ayllu governance which showed dramatically better 10-year sustainability; (5) documented research gap — no peer-reviewed study on sport-for-development with highland Nahua youth exists

- **Program Design Document** (`Sports_Research_Program_Design.md`) — comprehensive 13-section document synthesizing all research: vision/mission, target population, theory of change, sport selection rationale, scholar-athlete model, age-stage design (Tlapaloa 12–15, Icuilioa 15–18, Tlahtoa 18–22), coaching architecture, ejido governance model, opportunity pathways, nutrition/health integration, measurement framework, budget (MXN $110,000–$165,000 year 1 for 30 athletes), 4-phase plan with numbered steps
