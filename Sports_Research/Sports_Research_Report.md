- **Swimming:** Pool ($500K–$10M) disqualifies rural programs

A wrestling/boxing/judo combo room + running program for 60 athletes:
**~$15,000–$25,000 USD total** — realistic for a single municipal sports budget line.

## PART 2 — NAHUA / MACEHUALI YOUTH PHYSIOLOGY

### 2.1 Anthropometric Profile

| Metric | Nahua Men (est.) | Nahua Women (est.) | National MX Average (M) |
|--------|-----------------|-------------------|------------------------|
| Height | 158–164 cm | 147–153 cm | 167–169 cm |
| Weight | 60–70 kg | 52–62 kg | 72–76 kg |
| BMI | 22–26 | 23–27 | 25–28 |

**Key morphological traits:**
- **Longer torso / shorter legs** (Brachyskelic body type) → lower center of gravity
- Lean muscle-to-bodyweight ratio: relatively HIGH despite smaller absolute frame
- Well-developed calves and trapezius from lifetime terrain walking + mecapal load-carrying
- Bone structure: historically robust (bioarchaeological data); modern deficiency risks (Vit D, calcium)

**Regional variation:**
- Sierra de Zongolica (VZ, 1,200–2,700 m): most compact, mesomorphic builds
- Huasteca Hidalguense (lower elevation): slightly taller, more protein access historically
- Urban-adjacent communities: secular height gain ~2–4 cm per generation

### 2.2 Key Physiological Strengths

#### Altitude Adaptation (Highland Communities) — HIGH CONFIDENCE
- Zongolica (VZ): up to 2,700 m | Sierra Alta Hidalgo: 1,400–2,100 m
- Natural hematological adaptations: higher red blood cell mass, hemoglobin ~1–2 g/dL above sea-level norms, larger lung volumes
- Elite athletes pay to train at Flagstaff (2,100 m) / Iten Kenya (2,400 m) to achieve this
- Highland Nahua youth have it at **baseline, before any training**

#### Estimated VO2 Max (Untrained)
| Subgroup | Estimated VO2 max (males 18–30) |
|----------|--------------------------------|
| Highland Nahua (>1,500 m) | 48–58 mL/kg/min |
| Lowland Nahua (<500 m) | 42–50 mL/kg/min |
| Sedentary urban comparison | 35–42 mL/kg/min |
| Elite endurance athlete (reference) | 65–80 mL/kg/min |

#### Muscle Fiber Profile (Inferred)
- Likely **mixed Type I / Type IIa** — not pure sprint (Type IIx) dominant
- Highland communities: slightly Type I-biased (endurance)
- Lowland communities: more balanced
- **Best fit:** Power-endurance sports (boxing, wrestling, judo) + middle-distance running; less suited to pure 100m sprint

#### Grip Strength / Explosive Power
- Grip: ~38–48 kg (right hand), at or above national average *relative to stature*
- From mecapal carrying, milpa fieldwork, construction labor

### 2.3 Historical Athletic Traditions

| Tradition | Physical Demands | Modern Sport Parallel |
|-----------|-----------------|----------------------|
| **Tlachtli / Ulama** (ball game) | Hip rotation, lateral agility, sustained aerobic effort, impact absorption | Judo throws, wrestling, volleyball |
| **Messenger running** (Aztec relay networks) | Institutionalized endurance; relay segments 4–5 km | Middle/long distance running |
| **Mecapal load-carrying** | 30–60 kg over mountain terrain; trapezius, calves, spinal endurance | Race walking, loaded endurance |
| **Chinampa / paddling (coastal VZ)** | Shoulder, lats, core rotation | Canoe/kayak sprint |
| **Ceremonial dances** | Balance, body control, rhythmic coordination | Gymnastics, combat sports |

### 2.4 Closest Athletic Parallel: Rarámuri (Tarahumara)

| Trait | Rarámuri | Highland Nahua | Confidence |
|-------|----------|---------------|------------|
| Stature (adult men) | 162–165 cm | 158–164 cm | Moderate |
| Maize-based diet | Yes | Yes | High |
| Altitude habitation | 1,500–2,400 m | 1,200–2,700 m | High |
| Endurance labor tradition | Yes (running + farming) | Yes (walking/carrying + farming) | High |
| VO2 max (untrained) | ~50–55 | ~48–55 (est.) | Low |
| Running culture (continuous living tradition) | **YES — central** | Historical, largely lost | High |

**Key difference:** Rarámuri have a continuous ultra-running culture; Nahua communities must rebuild it. This is a program design gap, not a physiological gap.

## PART 4 — CRITICAL WARNING: NUTRITION AND STUNTING

### The Problem
- **34–44% stunting rate** in indigenous Mexican children vs. ~13% national average (ENSANUT 2012)
- Stunting **permanently** reduces adult height 5–12 cm — this explains the entire height gap
- **Iron deficiency anemia** in 20–35% of women → masks true aerobic capacity on any fitness test
- Nutrition transition (Coca-Cola, Bimbo, Maruchan replacing traditional diet) accelerating

### Program Must-Dos
1. **Iron screening before fitness testing** — child with Hgb <11 g/dL tests falsely low on everything aerobic
2. **Bone age X-ray (wrist) for talent screening** — distinguishes genetic short stature (ideal for weight-class sports) from stunting (needs nutritional intervention)
3. **Restore traditional food access** — quelites (wild greens: quintonil, verdolaga, huauzontle) are extraordinarily rich in iron, calcium, omega-3, folate
4. **Partner with DIF/BIENESTAR** nutrition programs already operating in communities
5. **Target ages 6–14** — before puberty closes growth plates; after stunting window (0–5) has passed

### The Paradox: Traditional Diet is Actually Good
The milpa diet (maize + beans + quelites + chilis) is a high-complex-carb, complete-protein, micronutrient-rich athletic diet. The problem is its erosion, not the diet itself.

## PART 5 — STRATEGIC RECOMMENDATIONS

### Immediate Launch (Near-Zero Infrastructure)
1. **Running club** (middle/long distance) — target highland communities first for altitude advantage
2. **Track & field basics** (sprints, long jump, shot put) — flat area + shared implements only

### Phase 2 — Community Space Partnership
3. **Multi-combat-sport room** — one mat set + shared boxing/judo gear = wrestling + boxing + judo simultaneously
4. **Taekwondo** — contact state federation (Xalapa / Pachuca); they often provide startup mats + coaching

### Phase 3 — When Budget Allows
5. **Weightlifting setup** (~$1,500–$2,000 for barbell + plates + plywood platform)
6. **Cycling** (if mountain terrain communities — altitude advantage is real)

### CONADE Assets to Leverage
- **Becas CONADE:** Once athlete reaches national junior level → $300–$600 USD/month stipend
- **IDERV (Veracruz) / CODEH (Hidalgo):** State institutes co-fund coaches
- **EIDM (Escuelas de Iniciación Deportiva Municipal):** CONADE grants equipment to qualified municipalities
- **Federación Mexicana de Taekwondo:** Most proactive federation at reaching rural municipalities

### The Reference Model
**Colombia/Ecuador Andes → cycling/running success:**
- Rural mountain communities, low per-capita income
- Children who walk/run long distances to school → natural endurance base
- Local coaches (former athletes) working for minimal pay
- Municipal government funds shoes + entry fees only
- Richard Carapaz (Ecuador, cycling Olympic gold 2020), Jefferson Pérez (race walking gold 1996) — both from poor rural Andean communities
- Mexico has the CONADE infrastructure; **the gap is local mobilization**
